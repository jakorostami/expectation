# Three certified constructions for the sequential e-testing engine

Status: IMPLEMENTED (2026-09-11), plan v4 + reference audit; see Review. Bar applied: a foreign theorem must do real
work, the result must be an object the e-value world lacks, validity must be enforced by an
exactly computable certificate, and it must land at a specific place in this engine.

## Reference audit (user-requested; changes the novelty labels, not the constructions)

Searched the e-statistics literature for prior work on A, B, C. Findings, read this session:
- B is substantially anticipated. Pérez-Ortiz, Castro & Stoepker (2025; arXiv:2506.22588,
  v2 2026), "Anytime-Valid Tests for Sparse Anomalies": oracle test martingale
  E_t = prod_i {(1-eps) + eps LR_i(t)} (their Eq. 4), adaptive mixture over a discrete
  (eps, delta) grid (Sec. 2.2), detection moment t* = T* rho(beta) (Thm 2.1, 2.3, 2.6, 2.7).
  Also e-HC (OpenReview B4mu5A3wVN, 2025; authors not retrievable here) for p-value streams.
  Filtration contract: Wang, Dandapanthula & Ramdas (2025; arXiv:2502.08539) — local
  e-processes are global under independence across streams / a causal condition.
  => B is reframed as: PCS's construction in the engine with *pluggable per-stream
  e-processes* (their Gaussian LR grid is one instance; the library's mixture martingales
  absorb the delta-mixture), plus localisation diagnostics; their t* verified numerically.
- C is substantially anticipated. Martinez-Taboada & Ramdas (AISTATS 2025, PMLR 258;
  arXiv:2409.17505), "Sequential Kernelized Stein Discrepancy": payoff
  g_t(x) = sum_i h_p(X_i, x) / sum_i M_p(X_i) with per-point lower bounds
  M_p(x~) >= -inf_x h_p(x, x~), lambda in [0,1], aGRAPA/LBOW (Sec. 4, Thm 1, Thm 2); bounds
  derived per model for the IMQ kernel (Sec. 5); Zhou & Liu (2024) earlier with bounded kernels.
  => C is reframed as: SKSD in the engine, plus (i) a *generic closed-form* M_p from a Lipschitz
  constant of the score, valid for IMQ and for compactly supported Wendland kernels (their
  Sec. 5.1 structure with Gamma(u) = L u), and (ii) the compact-support variant under which
  E_p[h_p(X, y)] = 0 holds for every C^1 positive density with no moment condition
  (SKSD Thm 1 needs E[h_p(X,X)] < inf). Trade-off to document: compact support forgoes the
  tail-detection advantages of IMQ (Gorham & Mackey 2017).
- A sits inside a general theory but its computational layer is not found elsewhere.
  Larsson, Ramdas & Ruf (2025; arXiv:2504.02974), "Testing hypotheses generated by
  constraints": representation theorem for all e-variables of constraint-generated
  hypotheses (finitely many constraints, sub-psi, group symmetries; moments, quantiles,
  CVaR); Clerico (2024/25; arXiv:2412.21125) optimal e-variable sets for finitely many
  regular constraints; Ram, Larsson, Ruf & Ramdas (2026; arXiv:2606.24768) strong duality
  for GROW (information-projection pairs, GROW value = relative entropy for bounded
  e-variables); Larsson, Ramdas & Ruf, numeraire/RIPr (Ann. Statist. 2025). Fixed-sample
  Wasserstein robust testing: Gao, Xie, Xie & Xu (NeurIPS 2018). No e-process work found on
  Wasserstein-ball or Huber-contamination nulls, nor on LP-certified support functions.
  => A keeps its framing: the exact, certified, finite-outcome instantiation (LP support
  functions, Huber–Strassen recovery, Kantorovich-potential witness, EM RIPr, sequential
  plug-in, W1 distance CS) of that theory, credited to it.
- Set-aside item also anticipated: Koning (2026, JRSSB), "Anytime validity is free:
  inducing sequential tests" covers lifting fixed tests to sequential ones.

Honest novelty labels after audit: A = computational instantiation with certificates
(moderate); B = implementation of PCS 2025 + pluggable streams + diagnostics (modest);
C = implementation of SKSD 2025 + generic Lipschitz certificate + compact-support variant
(modest). All three remain capabilities this engine lacks.

Ground rules: new self-contained modules; log-space state; frozen Pydantic configs/results;
ABCs at extension points; NumPy/SciPy only; no dependence on audited-defective paths; the
library's `EProcess`/`EProcessUpdater` combiners do temporal betting; tests use exact checks
wherever the object is finite, Monte Carlo otherwise.

## A — Upper-expectation nulls on finite outcome spaces, with certified log-optimal e-variables
Transfer: GTP upper expectations (Shafer & Vovk 2019) + Huber–Strassen least-favourable pairs
(1973, Ann. Statist.) + Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality / Wasserstein DRO + vertex reduction and
RIPr (Grünwald, de Heide & Koolen 2024 JRSSB; Larsson, Ramdas & Ruf 2024; Csiszár & Tusnády
1984). New object: e-variables valid for a whole uncertainty set, certified by an exactly
computed support function, with the set's own duality supplying Skeptic's optimal strategy.

Math (finite outcome space of size K, null set 𝒫, e-variable E >= 0):
- Validity iff sigma_𝒫(E) := sup_{P in 𝒫} <P, E> <= 1. Certify: E <- E / max(1, sigma_𝒫(E)).
- Contamination 𝒫 = {(1-eps) P0 + eps H}: sigma = (1-eps)<P0,E> + eps max E (closed form).
  Optimal vs belief q: E* = min(q/P0, c)/Z with (c, Z) from 1-D root finding so sigma = 1;
  verified against a generic convex programme (maximise <q, log E> s.t. sigma <= 1).
- Wasserstein 𝒫 = {Q : W1(Q, P0) <= rho} with cost matrix C: sigma via transport LP
  (`scipy.optimize.linprog`, HiGHS). Witness form: for 1-Lipschitz h (w.r.t. C),
  E = 1 + lam [ (h - <P0,h>) - rho ] has <Q,E> <= 1 on the ball since <Q,h> - <P0,h> <= rho.
  Optimal h = Kantorovich potential from the LP dual between q and P0; lam by 1-D log-optimal
  search on [0, 1/(rho + <P0,h> - min h)]. Growth possible iff W1(q, P0) > rho.
- Polytope 𝒫 = conv{P_v}: sigma = max_v <P_v, E>. Optimal E* = q/p*, p* = RIPr of q by
  mixture-weight EM; certified. CHSH: 16 local-deterministic vertices; singlet at Tsirelson
  angles gives S = 2 sqrt 2 and KL(q||p*) > 0; local vertices give |S| <= 2, KL = 0, E ≡ 1.
- Sequential: belief q_{t-1} = KT-smoothed empirical law (predictable) or fixed alternative;
  E_t certified each step; test supermartingale under any adapted choice P_t in 𝒫.
- Distance CS: run the Wasserstein Skeptic on a grid of rho; the rejected set is {rho < rho_t}
  (tax monotone in rho) -> anytime-valid lower confidence bound on W1(Reality, P0).

## B — Sparsity-adaptive global-null e-process for many independent streams
Transfer: Ingster's sparse-mixture likelihood ratio (1997) and the Donoho–Jin detection
boundary (2004, Ann. Statist.); the freezing/participation-ratio picture of Derrida's random
energy model as interpretation only. New object: mixture over sparsity of the library's
lambda-product merge applied to e-*processes* in time, giving an anytime-valid global test
that is powerful exactly where the arithmetic-mean merge is powerless.

Math: streams i = 1..K independent, each M_i(t) a test supermartingale under its null.
F_eps(t) = prod_i [(1-eps) + eps M_i(t)] is a nonnegative supermartingale (product of
independent nonnegative supermartingales); E_t = sum_j pi_j F_{eps_j}(t) is one too;
Ville applies. Posterior over eps and responsibilities r_i = eps M_i/((1-eps)+eps M_i)
localise evidence; participation ratio (sum r_i)^2 / sum r_i^2 is the diagnostic.
Contract: independence across streams (same as `ProductMerger`).

      K = 1e5, single step, beta in {0.6, 0.8}, one r clearly below and one clearly above
      rho*(beta); planted streams get top responsibilities.

## C — Stein tickets against a Forecaster who can only announce a score
Transfer: Stein's identity / kernel Stein discrepancy (Gorham & Mackey 2015; Liu, Lee &
Jordan 2016; Chwialkowski, Strathmann & Gretton 2016) + compactly supported Wendland kernels
(1995). New object: a certified bounded zero-mean payoff for unnormalised targets, so the
library's combiners can bet on goodness of fit without a partition function.

Math: k_p(x,y) = s(x).s(y) k + s(x).grad_y k + s(y).grad_x k + sum_i d_{x_i} d_{y_i} k with
k(x,y) = phi(||x-y||/r), phi Wendland of order l = floor(d/2)+2. E_p[k_p(X,y)] = 0 for every C^1
positive density p (divergence theorem on the compact support). With ||s(x)-s(y)|| <= L||x-y||:
|k_p(x,y)| <= B(y) = (||s(y)||+Lr)||s(y)|| K0 + (2||s(y)||+Lr) K1 + K2,
K0 = 1, K1 = (l+2)(l/(l+1))^l / r, K2 = (l+1)(l+2) d / r^2. Witness g_t(x) = mean_{i<t}
k_p(x, X_i), bound B_t = mean_i B(X_i) (predictable). Emit E_t = 1 + g_t(X_t)/B_t in [0,2],
conditional mean exactly 1 under H0 (X_t | F_{t-1} ~ p); combiners bet 1 + lambda g_t/B_t.
E_q[g_t] -> KSD^2(q,p) >= 0. Null caveat: conditional law p; autocorrelated MCMC violates it.

Code-review rework (user feedback): sparse mixture moved from its own module into `merging.py`
as a merging function; Bell helpers folded into `CHSHLocalRealismNull(PolytopeNull)`; config
models folded into `skeptic.py` modules; abstract-property stacks replaced by plain attributes
and abstract methods; targets are `ScoreTarget` subclasses implementing `_score`; distance

Deviations from plan, all deliberate:
- Protocol orchestrator named `BettingProtocol` (not `TestingProtocol`: pytest collects `Test*`).
- `SkepticStrategy` has a two-phase `bet`/`observe` contract so a rejected e-value leaves
  strategy state untouched (avoids the RIPr-02 class of defect).
- No scalar ONS rule was added: the library's `EmpiricallyAdaptiveCombiner` already is the
  GRAPA-type bet on `g = E - 1`, so the Stein Skeptic emits `E_t = 1 + g_t` and reuses it.
- Sparse-mixture participation ratio uses positive per-stream log contributions (zero at
  capital one) rather than responsibilities (which equal eps for a null-looking stream).
- Wasserstein optimal e-variable skips the LP certificate: validity is exact by
  Kantorovich-Rubinstein plus a violation tax for the potential's LP tolerance.
- Polytope membership is read from the RIPr KL (< 1e-9 nats) instead of a separate LP.

Evidence, A (upperexp; 39 tests):
- Contamination support function equals the point-mass-adversary brute force to 1e-12;
  certified e satisfies `<Q, e> <= 1` for 200 random contaminants exactly.
- Optimal e-variable has the Huber-Strassen form `min(r/kappa, m)` (constant kappa on the
  unclipped set) and its growth equals a generic SLSQP GRO solution to 1e-6.
- Wasserstein: LP support function matches the two-point closed form; `W1` matches the 1-D
  CDF formula to 1e-9; the dual potential is 1-Lipschitz and attains `W1`; witness growth
  equals the generic GRO to 1e-4 (the witness family is numerically optimal on K=3);
  growth > 0 iff `W1(q, P0) > rho`.
- Polytope: RIPr KL equals direct SLSQP KL minimisation to 1e-7; certified `q/p*` satisfies
  every vertex; growth equals KL. CHSH: all 16 local vertices have |S| = 2, 50 random local
  mixtures have |S| <= 2, singlet at Tsirelson angles has |S| = 2 sqrt 2 and KL > 0.01,
  local mixtures have KL = 0 and `e ≡ 1`.
- Sequential: under an adversary choosing the least favourable set member after seeing the
  bet (all three sets), mean final capital <= 1 within 3 s.e. and time-uniform rejections
  within the binomial band at alpha = 0.1; power outside each set; per-round bid-ask
  announcements work; plug-in Skeptic detects the singlet without knowing it; quantum data
  grow at the KL rate (within 25% over 1500 rounds); Wasserstein distance sequence covers
  time-uniformly and its bound rises toward the true distance.

Evidence, B (sparsemixture; 11 tests):
- Single-eps output equals `LambdaProductMerger` to 1e-12; mixture equals the prior average
  of lambda-products to 1e-12 relative.
- Global null with 300 independent streams driven by `OneSidedNormalMixture` (known unit
  variance): mean capital <= 1 within 3 s.e., time-uniform rejections within band.
- 10 planted streams at e = 1000 among 20,000: arithmetic-mean merge < 20 (no rejection at
  alpha = 0.05), sparse mixture rejects, planted streams are the top-10, participation ratio ≈ 10.
- PCS detection moment: K = 2000, beta = 0.75, T* = 40 (t* = 10): cumulative rejection by
  t = 3 was <= 25%, by t = 40 >= 85% over 20 replications.

Evidence, C (stein; 24 tests):
- IMQ and Wendland radial derivatives match finite differences; `psi'(u)/u` finite at 0.
- Stein identity by quadrature: |E_p h_p(X, y)| < 1e-7 for N(0.3, 1.5) and for the double-well
  Boltzmann density (score not globally Lipschitz), three reference points each, both kernels;
  2-D Gaussian Monte Carlo mean within 4 s.e. of zero.
- Kernel constants dominate 200,001-point grid maxima for IMQ (d = 1, 2, 5) and Wendland
  (d = 1, 2, 3, 6); certified `M_p(y)` bounds `-min_x h_p(x, y)` on 60,001-120,001-point grids
  for Gaussian (both kernels) and double-well with local Lipschitz (Wendland).
- Our IMQ Gaussian constant `y^2 + y + 0.587` is below SKSD's hand-derived `y(1+y) + 3`.
- Through the protocol with the empirically adaptive combiner: time-uniform type-I within
  band for both kernels (100 reps x 60 rounds); power against a unit mean shift (both
  kernels) and against t(2) tails (IMQ); double-well Boltzmann target with local Lipschitz
  and Wendland kernel: exact null draws keep capital <= 1, a shifted sampler is rejected.

hypothesis / GROW theory for three concrete sets (moderate); B = implementation of
Pérez-Ortiz, Castro & Stoepker (2025) with pluggable streams and diagnostics (modest);
C = implementation of Martinez-Taboada & Ramdas (2025) with generic Lipschitz certificates
and a compact-support variant (modest).
